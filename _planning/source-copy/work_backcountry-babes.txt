h1: Rebuilding Sponsor Relationships
div: Backcountry Babes
div: The Challenge
div: Backcountry Babes was losing sponsors and underutilizing current sponsor relationships. We needed to better articulate who we were to stoke excitement with sponsors to reap the benefits of those relationships.
div: Our Solution
div: I created a small printed portfolio to share with sponsors during annual in-person meetings at the Outdoor Retailer conference.
div: My Contributions
div: This was a solo project I did as one of the co-founders of Constellation Outdoor Education. I conducted informal stakeholder interviews with current and potential sponsors. I synthesized the research and created the deliverables.

div: Backcountry Babes clients hiking uphill to ski in Silverton, CO.
div: Unprecedented success, but losing sponsors
p: After my company, Constellation Outdoor Education, acquired Backcountry Babes we overhauled the brand, website and social accounts. This change resulted in a significant increase in enrollment and community engagement, but at the same time we saw partnerships decline. ‍ For a small outdoor experience company, relationships with sponsors are vital to extending the advertising capabilities of a limited budget. Why were we losing sponsors when by all other metrics the company was doing so well?
h1: Uncovering Barriers to Trust
p: Though we had become experts at understanding who our clients were, our sponsors were still a mystery to us, invisible to us except for a couple emails and boxes (or pallets) of product that showed up unpredictably. We made assumptions about what being a good partner looked liked.
p: Maybe it was lot of tagging on social media, maybe it was talking up their brands on our courses or using their products exclusively or posting about them on our blog. I reached out to a handful of our sponsors, friends that were brand reps for outdoor companies and cold called a few brand reps to ask about what it would take to bring them on board as sponsors.
div: INSIGHT 1
div: We needed to be proactive about educating our sponsors about who we were, what we were doing, and speaking their langauge.
div: INSIGHT 3
div: People were excited about our social justice mission to reduce the gender disparity in outdoor leadership, our access to female skiers, and our high levels of trust and engagement.
div: INSIGHT 2
div: Every brand has a different strategy and to uncover each one we needed to drive conversations about the two-way value promise of partnering tailored to that sponsor’s goals and needs.

div: Make space to co-create future partnerships. Seek input about sponsor needs and articulate what we offer.
div: OBJECTIVE 2
div: Paint a vivid picture of our two distinct client types, our mission, and our unique positioning in the market.
div: OBJECTIVE 3
div: Create a physical artifact to be paired with an in-person meeting guided by a conversation plan. Not just another email or blog post.
div: Speaking their Language
div: BUILDING Profiles of our Bimodal Client Base
p: We created two detailed personas based on our intimate knowledge of the Backcountry Babes clients. One persona describes a new client who is getting into the sport who needed guidance on buying new gear ("Getting Stoked"). The other is a long-time skier who loves having the best gear ("Still Stoked"). Reps were able to recognize our clients as customers that would be valuable to their brand.

div: LETTING OUR DATA DO THE TALKING
div: We developed a social media dashboard and participation dashboard to show off our high levels of organic engagement online and the reach of our high-touch in-person events. These resonated with brand reps who live and die by similar metrics. ‍

div: A NEW plan for driving the conversation
div: We realized that busy brand partners didn’t often take the lead in defining the terms of partnerships. We had to be the ones in the driver’s seat, both through articulating what we wanted from them and showing off what we could offer. ‍ We used feedback from existing and potential sponsors to refine our materials to the topics and data brand reps found most compelling. These were an affinitized list of what we could offer to brands and mockups of how we were sharing sponsor content on our social media platforms and website. ‍ The tools drove a conversation about each brand’s unique values. We used these as a jumping off point to understand what a partnership might look like and build excitement about the places that our company could support the work that reps were doing. ‍
