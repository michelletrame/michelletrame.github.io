If you're interested in where adventure and design meet, read my Medium post about how working as an outdoor guide shapes my work as a designer.

My design ethos

Catching falling fruit.

Collaboration is at the core of my values. In my early twenties, living in a Santa Cruz house with probably too many people and even more bikes, I met a group who were gleaning: collecting fruit from trees in the medians of busy boulevards, in front yards and in tiny backyard orchards.

The goal was to intercept food (with permission) that would otherwise go to waste and turn it into something delicious, nourishing and useful. We shared the fresh fruit with friends and food banks, and made ciders, preserves and fruit butters. It felt radical.

It opened my eyes to how much abundance is around us that we fail to use. As a designer, I aim to capture the goodness that is right at hand. Whether in small details or complex systems, I work in the space of things beyond our notice, or that we haven't yet put to use, to create enduringly joyful products, services and experiences.
