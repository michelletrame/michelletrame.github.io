Research

When the playbook fails

We talked to gig-economy workers to see how their financial habits differed from other people's. Most had turned to gig work after a financial setback, without a safety net to fall back on.

For most, gig work was just one of the strategies they leaned on when times were tough. We heard about selling possessions, cutting back dramatically, taking out predatory loans, using debt consolidation or credit repair services, and borrowing from friends and family. Each was emotional in its own way, but the shame and anxiety of borrowing from friends and family was especially acute.

Going deeper

Zooming in on borrowing and lending

We ran more research into the specific problems of borrowing and lending money, and built simple models of what a service might look like and how it could help borrowers and lenders.

{% include figure.html src="vouch/vouch-illustrations" diagram=true alt="Three line drawings used as research prompts: two friends talking over coffee, a hand holding a phone showing a payment reminder, and two phones exchanging a loan request." caption="We shared simple vignettes with people who were borrowing from or lending to friends and family, to spark conversations about the tools they use, what would help and where it hurts." %}

We also looked at who was already serving people who need to borrow: peer-to-peer lending between strangers, micro-loans and payday lending.

  • $183BAbout 88 million Americans lend roughly $183 billion a year to friends and family.
  • 46%of people who have lent money to friends or family say it ended badly: a damaged relationship, not being paid back, or both.
  • 4% vs 700%A typical loan between friends or family carries about 4% interest. Payday lenders can charge up to 700%.

Could we support these loans so that people didn't have to choose between their financial health and the health of their relationships?

Insights

Creating conditions for success

We developed three insights about how borrowing becomes a problem, and a design principle to answer each.

  1. People feel shame and embarrassment about borrowing money.→Build borrower and lender confidence.
  2. Uncomfortable power dynamics make people avoid talking about loans.→Compel proactive communication.
  3. People are anxious about damaging their relationships.→Foster support and flexibility.

Pilot and prototypes

Eight iterations, each one tested with people

We ran a pilot that brought in real users at every stage, while building prototypes to test with them and with new participants. Each round focused on something different: communicating the value, building trust, usability or brand identity.

  1. Landing page v1

    We made
    A simple landing page to gauge interest in the product.
    We learned
    People needed more clarity about what the product was and who it was for. Conversion from Google ads was surprisingly high.
    {% include figure.html src="vouch/vouch-landing-v1" alt="First landing page, headlined Money Lending Made Easy, with a phone showing the Vouch logo, an email sign-up and four feature blurbs." %}

  2. MVP onboarding

    We made
    An onboarding form for Vouch users.
    We learned
    People didn't know what to expect at the end, and we needed to build more trust before asking them to sign up. Some questions were confusing, and splitting users into four paths with their own questions was an unnecessary complication.
    {% include figure.html src="vouch/onboarding" alt="Four screens from a step-by-step onboarding form: a welcome screen, then questions asking for the person's name, how much of the loan they've repaid and whether they've discussed the loan before." %}

  3. MVP deliverables

    We made
    A first term sheet: a summary of the loan's terms, a repayment schedule, and other concerns to consider.
    We learned
    People valued it because it was simpler than an amortization schedule and felt more personal, something to bring into a conversation with their lender or borrower. They wanted to be able to change it if circumstances changed.
    {% include figure.html src="vouch/template" alt="A term sheet template titled Borrower and Lender's Loan, with a loan overview, a plain-language summary of terms, things to consider and a repayment table." %}

  4. Landing page v2

    We made
    A landing page explaining Vouch, its promises and features, with an option to onboard a loan with us.
    We learned
    People were still unsure what we offered, why it was valuable and who it was for. Some phrases resonated, like "Turn a hard conversation into a simple request." People hated the idea of scheduling a phone call to onboard.
    {% include figure.html src="vouch/vouch-landing-v2" alt="Second landing page, headlined Borrow Money, Build Trust over a painted texture, with three features: You're in charge, Stay on track and Stay on the same page." %}

  5. Concierge onboarding

    We made
    Onboarding by scheduled phone call with a Vouch loan coach.
    We learned
    People didn't arrive with a clear idea of the loan they wanted. They wanted to move numbers around to compare options, and to know what's normal. I started running calls with a spreadsheet so I could mock up scenarios, though explaining them over the phone wasn't ideal. One of those back-of-the-napkin spreadsheets became the first draft of our loan calculator.
    {% include figure.html src="vouch/loan-calc" alt="A spreadsheet with color-coded blocks comparing loan scenarios with and without interest: principal, payment interval, payment amount, number of payments and time to pay off." %}

  6. Wireframes

    We made
    Wireframes and clickable prototypes that turned our concierge experience into a mobile web platform, so onboarding could scale.
    We learned
    People trusted the app version more than the form, and were happy to give up some control for simplicity. Older people wanted to use it on a desktop. Younger testers cited app fatigue, especially for a tool that relies on inviting someone else to use it too.
    {% include figure.html src="vouch/wireframes" alt="A large map of mobile wireframes connected in a flow, covering onboarding, building a loan and the loan dashboard." %}

  7. Landing page v3

    We made
    A landing page explaining the service, with options to onboard by email, form or scheduled call.
    We learned
    The value and the process were finally clear. People responded differently to stock photos and illustrations; with more resources we'd invest in visuals that fit better.
    {% include figure.html src="vouch/vouch-landing-v3" alt="Third landing page: Borrowing money from friends and family? We can help. An illustration shows two people talking at a table, above the line Borrow responsibly. Borrow with confidence." %}

  8. First mocks

    We made
    Medium-fidelity mocks to repeat our wireframe usability tests while asking about look and feel. Is it friendly? Supportive? Easy? Who would use this?
    We learned
    The interface was intuitive but dry, and didn't carry the tone we wanted. People felt it looked like any other banking app.
    {% include figure.html src="vouch/version1" alt="A medium-fidelity Your Loans screen with a button to create a new loan request, a loan between Hazel and Eric, and tools including a loan calculator and lending scenarios." %}

Design

Falling in love with the problem

{% include figure.html src="vouch/designing" alt="Two teammates sketch on a whiteboard wall in a bright studio." %}

User testing guided each new iteration, but our North Star was the design principles we identified early on. We revisited and updated them regularly, treating them as both sacred and malleable. We looked at banking and lending apps to see how others solved similar problems, but our research had given us an acute sense of what goes wrong when people lend and borrow, and we let that inform every step.

One of our mentors said the most remarkable thing about our work was our team's ability to fall in love with the problem, but not with our solution.

Principle 1

Building borrower and lender confidence

The loan calculator is the first step in creating a loan request. It reduces stress by showing borrowers how the interest rate, repayment amount and timeframe relate to each other.

We start with what the borrower needs to succeed: how much, what they can repay and when, and interest if that matters to them. Then we give them a plan.

{% include figure.html src="vouch/loan-builders-new" alt="The Build your ideal loan screen: loan amount, repayment frequency and amount, an interest slider, and a summary of interest to the lender, the total repaid and the number of payments." %}
Principle 2

Compelling proactive communication

People wanted a tool that would help with the money and also with the tough interpersonal dynamics. Onboarding prompts both borrower and lender to decide how they want to communicate over the life of the loan.

When they check the status of the loan, the first thing they see are the agreements they made when they created it.

{% include figure.html src="vouch/agreements" alt="A screen asking What agreements are important to you as a borrower?, with options such as Talk in person if there are any concerns, Provide context for any changes and Address issues directly." %}
Principle 3

Fostering support and flexibility

Flexibility is a big part of what makes lending between friends and family different. Borrowers can repay early or change their schedule without penalties, and any change starts a conversation between borrower and lender, so there are no surprises.

Lenders told us they hated having to remind borrowers about payments, so Vouch sends the reminders and follows up on missed payments.

Where it went

The current state of Vouch

My co-founders and I continued working on Vouch, exploring what becomes possible when we challenge long-held assumptions about lending money to friends and family, and the myth of rugged individualism.
