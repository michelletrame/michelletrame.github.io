Context

Unprecedented success, but losing sponsors

After my company, Constellation Outdoor Education, acquired Backcountry Babes, we overhauled the brand, website and social accounts. Enrollment and community engagement rose significantly, but at the same time our partnerships declined.

For a small outdoor company, sponsors are vital to stretching a limited marketing budget. Why were we losing them when every other metric looked so good?

Research

Uncovering barriers to trust

We'd become experts on our clients, but our sponsors were a mystery: invisible except for a few emails and the boxes (or pallets) of product that showed up unpredictably. We'd made assumptions about what being a good partner looked like.

Maybe it was lots of tagging on social media, talking up their brands on our courses, using their products exclusively, or posting about them on our blog. I reached out to some of our sponsors and to friends who were brand reps for outdoor companies, and cold-called a few more reps to ask what it would take to bring them on as sponsors.

Objectives

A successful solution will…

  1. Make space to co-create future partnerships: ask what sponsors need, and say clearly what we offer.
  2. Paint a vivid picture of our two distinct client types, our mission and our unique place in the market.
  3. Be a physical artifact paired with an in-person meeting and a conversation plan. Not just another email or blog post.

Speaking their language

Profiles of our two kinds of clients

We created two detailed personas based on our close knowledge of Backcountry Babes clients: a newcomer to the sport who needs guidance buying gear ("Getting Stoked"), and a longtime skier who loves having the best gear ("Still Stoked"). Brand reps recognized both as customers who'd be valuable to their brands.

Letting our data do the talking

We built a social media dashboard and a participation dashboard to show our high organic engagement online and the reach of our hands-on, in-person events. They resonated with brand reps, who live and die by similar metrics.

Conversation plan

A new plan for driving the conversation

Busy brand partners rarely took the lead in defining partnerships. We had to be in the driver's seat, saying what we wanted from them and showing what we could offer.

Feedback from current and potential sponsors helped us narrow our materials to the topics and data reps found most compelling: an affinitized menu of what we could offer, and mock-ups of how we'd feature sponsor content on our social media and website. These tools opened a conversation about each brand's values, and became the starting point for imagining a partnership and building excitement about where we could support reps' work.

Digital opportunities

  • Logo and link in the website footer
  • Logo, description and link on the sponsor page
  • Logo, link and message or image in the newsletter
  • Links to your products on equipment lists
  • Sponsored social media post
  • Sponsored blog post (product review or educational)
  • Sponsored video (product review or educational)
  • Cross-posting content (yours or ours): blogs, videos
  • Logo in film festival or video content
  • Photography: trip or event (product feature)
  • Contest (on social media or in the newsletter)

In-person engagements

  • Logo on course posters in ski towns in California, Alaska and Colorado
  • Film festival sponsorship: logo on promotional materials, a mention from the host at screenings, logo on screen
  • Logo on the Backcountry Babes van, seen at the best ski destinations in North America
  • Access to our trips and courses for your photographer
  • Coupon codes handed out to participants
  • Branded items for participants (stickers, pencils, keychains, lip balm, ski straps)
  • Equipment demos on trips or courses
  • A demo fleet of gear for all trips and courses
  • Hosting demo day events
  • Hosting any of our events for a group you organize

Outcome

Renewed relationships and new opportunities

{% include figure.html src="backcountry-babes/observations" alt="A group of skiers in helmets and packs gathers among snowy pines, one reading from a notebook." %} {% include figure.html src="backcountry-babes/backcountry-splitboard-touring-breckenridge" alt="A line of splitboarders and skiers skins uphill through snow-laden trees near Breckenridge." %}

Existing sponsors

Shared inspiration and collaboration with existing sponsors shored up previously rocky relationships, rallied promotional support for our events and kept their material support coming.

New sponsorships

We won new sponsorships with notoriously choosy brands, and with boutique brands whose social justice missions aligned with ours: ending the gender gap in outdoor leadership.

New opportunities

We expanded our relationships with a top-shelf, highly respected ski company and a leading maker of avalanche safety equipment. That brought a fleet of demo skis for clients, and an educational video series featuring our guides, produced and distributed by the brand.
